An open-weight model is one whose trained parameters are released for anyone to download and run. You can host it yourself, fine-tune it, and keep all your data inside your network. It's narrower than open source, because the training data and code often aren't released.

Teams usually choose them for data control, cost at high volume, or customization. One healthcare company couldn't send patient notes to an external API, so it ran an open-weight model in its own cloud account, and passed its compliance review in weeks rather than months.

The trade-off is that you take on the operations: serving, capacity, patching and on-call. Confirm quality with your own evals, not public leaderboards. And read the license, because some restrict commercial use or apply to fine-tunes too.
