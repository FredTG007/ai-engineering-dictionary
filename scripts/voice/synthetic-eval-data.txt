Synthetic eval data means test cases written by a model instead of a person, then filtered until they are worth keeping. You use it when real labelled examples are rare, confidential, or slow to collect, and you need a set large enough to measure with.

The risk is that the generated set is easier than the real world. One team built two thousand synthetic cases in a day and celebrated a ninety-five percent pass rate. When they finally graded a hundred real tickets by hand, the pass rate was thirty percent. The generator had written tidy single questions. Real tickets were two paragraphs long with three questions at once.

So seed the generator with real structure, drop cases the model gets right with no context, remove near duplicates, and hand-grade a sample before you trust any number.
