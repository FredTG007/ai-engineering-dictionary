Semantic caching returns a stored answer when a new question is close in meaning to one already seen, even if the wording differs. You embed the query, search past queries, and if the closest match clears a threshold, you skip the model.

The appeal is the hit rate. One help bot went from about three percent with exact matching to around thirty-five percent. Then it served the answer for "how do I delete my account" to someone asking how to delete their team account, a different and far more destructive process. The embeddings were close. The right answers weren't.

So tune the threshold on real query pairs labelled same or different. Scope the cache by product, language or tenant, and leave out personal or time-sensitive questions. Track wrong hits, not just hit rate.
