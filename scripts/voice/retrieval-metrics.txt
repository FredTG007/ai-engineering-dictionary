Retrieval metrics measure the search half of a RAG system on its own. You take queries where you know the relevant documents, run retrieval, and score how many came back and how high they ranked.

The most useful is usually recall at k: the share of relevant chunks in the top k, where k is how many you actually send to the model. One team saw answer quality drop after changing their chunking. When they finally measured, recall at five had fallen from eighty-two to sixty-four percent. The new chunks had split tables from their headings.

Add a ranking-aware metric, like mean reciprocal rank, to see whether good results come first. And report numbers per query type, because a better average can hide a worse category.
