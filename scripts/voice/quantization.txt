Quantization reduces the numeric precision used to store a model's weights, for example from sixteen bits down to eight or four. That cuts memory by half or more, so the model fits on fewer GPUs and usually generates faster.

Teams reach for it when a model doesn't fit their hardware. One team self-hosting a seventy-billion-parameter model needed about a hundred and forty gigabytes of GPU memory, which meant two expensive cards. A four-bit version fit on one, halving serving cost. Later they found it made more arithmetic mistakes in their invoice-checking flow.

The quality cost is real but uneven. Eight-bit is usually close to lossless. Four-bit can hurt math, code and strict formats, and small models suffer more. So run your evals on both versions, focusing on the tasks most sensitive to precision.
