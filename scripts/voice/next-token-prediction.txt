Next-token prediction is the one task a language model is trained on. Given all the tokens so far, it outputs a probability for every possible next token. Answering a question or calling a tool is that single step repeated until something says stop.

That is why models can be confidently wrong. One team built a form-filling assistant for customer records. It kept filling in postcodes that looked right for the city but belonged to a different street, and about eight percent of a week's records were wrong. The model had learned what postcodes there usually look like, not which postcode goes with which address.

The habit is simple. Never ask the model to know something it cannot know. Put the real value in the prompt, or give it a lookup tool, and validate the result.
