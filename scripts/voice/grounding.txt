Grounding means tying a model's answer to specific evidence you supplied, so each claim can be traced to a source instead of the model's general training. A grounded answer is one you can check line by line against the documents.

The need is clearest after an embarrassing mistake. A benefits assistant told an employee that dental coverage included orthodontics for adults. The retrieved policy was right there in the context, and it said the opposite. Retrieval worked. Grounding failed.

So tell the model to answer only from the sources, cite the passage behind each claim, and say when the answer isn't there. Then verify: check the citations exist, and use a judge model to flag unsupported claims. Track a groundedness rate in your evals, alongside correctness.
