A reasoning model is trained to work through a problem step by step before answering. It does this by default, producing thinking tokens that may be hidden or summarized, and many let you set how much effort to spend.

They help most on problems with many dependent steps. One insurance-claims assistant kept missing exclusions buried in policy clauses. Switching its analysis step to a reasoning model roughly halved the error rate, with no prompt changes. But the time to first token jumped from under a second to over fifteen, cost per claim rose about fourfold, and old timeouts started killing good requests.

So use them where they measurably help. Route simple tasks like classification to a fast standard model. And skip detailed step-by-step instructions; state the goal, the constraints and the output format clearly.
