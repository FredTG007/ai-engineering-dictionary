Top-p sampling, also called nucleus sampling, limits which tokens the model can pick at each step. It keeps the most likely candidates until their combined probability reaches a threshold, and samples from that shortlist. When the model is confident, the list is short. When it's uncertain, it's longer.

It's easy to misuse alongside temperature. One team debugging odd product descriptions found someone had set temperature high and top-p low, trying to be creative but safe. The settings fought each other, and the text came out repetitive in places and bizarre in others. Resetting top-p and tuning only temperature fixed it.

For most work, leave top-p at the default and adjust temperature. Neither setting fixes accuracy, though. If the output is making things up, the problem is context or retrieval, not the sampler.
