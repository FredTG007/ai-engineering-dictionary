A fallback model is a second option your system uses when the first one can't answer. If the main model errors, times out or hits a rate limit, the request goes to another model, region or provider. The goal is to keep the feature working, even at slightly lower quality.

Teams usually add fallbacks after an outage. One meeting-notes product went dark for ninety minutes when its only provider had an incident. They added a chain: the same model in another region, then a smaller model from a second provider. The next incident caused a brief quality dip instead.

But models aren't drop-in substitutes. A prompt tuned for one can break formatting or tool calls on another, so run your evals against every model in the chain. And log which model served each request.
